\begin{abstract}
We outline Jamneshan and Tao's quantitative $U^3$ inverse theorem for
arbitrary finite abelian groups.  Their Fourier-analytic proof lifts a local
bilinear form to a covering group before integrating it to a local quadratic
phase.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Introduction}\label{sec:introductionFE}

The preceding talks connected Gowers norms with arithmetic progressions; in
particular, Talk~10 showed that repeated Cauchy--Schwarz controls four-term
progression counts by the $U^3$ norm \cite{GowersFourAPFE}.  Thus a set with
too few such progressions has a balanced function with large $U^3$ norm, and
an inverse theorem identifies the structure needed for a density increment.

The theorem below is uniform in $G$.  Earlier proofs covered odd-order groups
\cite[Theorem~2.7]{GreenTaoFE} and $G=\mathbb{F}_2^n$
\cite[Theorem~2.3]{SamorodnitskyFE}, but relied on features unavailable in
arbitrary torsion.

Write $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$,
$\xi\cdot x=\xi(x)$, and $e(\theta)=e^{2\pi i\theta}$.  For a finite
$S\subset\widehat G$, put
\[
 \|x\|_S=\sup_{\xi\in S}\|\xi\cdot x\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)=\{x\in G:\|x\|_S<\rho\}.
\]
A Bohr set is \emph{regular} if a small relative change in its radius produces
only a comparably small relative change in its cardinality.
For a map $\phi:U\to H$, where $U$ lies in an additive group, define its
additive derivative by $\partial_h\phi(x)=\phi(x)-\phi(x-h)$ whenever both
terms are defined.  The map is \emph{locally linear} if every second
derivative vanishes on its natural domain, and \emph{locally quadratic} if
every third derivative does.
Here and below, the constants implicit in $O(1)$ and in Vinogradov notation
are absolute.

\begin{theorem}[Locally quadratic inverse theorem, Theorem~1.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{thm:inverse-u3FE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, let $0<\eta\leq1/2$, and let
$f:G\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|f|\leq1$ and $\|f\|_{U^3(G)}\geq\eta$.  Then there
are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ satisfying
\[
 |S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)},
 \qquad
 \exp\bigl(-\eta^{-O(1)}\bigr)\ll\rho\leq\frac{1}{100},
\]
a locally quadratic map
$\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, and a map
$\xi:G\to\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \left|
  \mathbb{E}_{h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}
  f(x+h)e\bigl(-\phi(h)-\xi(x)\cdot h\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Section~\ref{sec:bilinear-formsFE} gives the lifting and integration tools.
Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-5FE} extracts a locally linear frequency map $M$,
Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-6FE} symmetrises $(h,x)\mapsto M(h)\cdot x$, and
Section~\ref{sec:theorem-1-6FE} integrates the resulting form and extracts the
remaining linear frequency.

\subsection{Globalising and integrating locally bilinear forms}
\label{sec:bilinear-formsFE}

The form constructed below is only local.  The results here lift it to
$G_S$, integrate it globally, and restrict the resulting quadratic map back
to a smaller Bohr set.

Let $U$ be a subset of an additive group and let $H$ be another additive
group.  A map $B:U\times U\to H$ is \emph{locally bilinear} if
$B(x_1+x_2,y)=B(x_1,y)+B(x_2,y)$ whenever all four arguments belong to $U$,
and likewise in the second variable.  It is \emph{symmetric} if
$B(x,y)=B(y,x)$.  For
$S\subset\widehat G$, define the lifted group
\[
 G_S=\left\{(x,\theta)\in G\times\mathbb{R}^S:
       (\xi\cdot x)_{\xi\in S}=\theta\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^S}\right\},
 \qquad
 \|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}=\sup_{\xi\in S}|\theta_\xi|.
\]
The following two results from
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE} will be used as black boxes.

\begin{lemma}[Globalising a locally bilinear form; Lemma~2.2]
\label{lem:globalisingFE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $S\subset\widehat G$, and
$0<\rho<1/2$.  If $B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\to H$ is locally
bilinear, then there is a globally bilinear map
$\widetilde B:G_S^2\to H$ such that
$\widetilde B((x,\theta),(y,\sigma))=B(x,y)$ whenever
$\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S},\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}
 \leq\exp(-C|S|^C)\rho$ for some absolute $C>0$.  If $B$ is symmetric,
$\widetilde B$ may also be chosen symmetric.
\end{lemma}

The lift is genuinely needed: already for a cyclic group there are locally
bilinear forms on a Bohr set that do not extend to all of $G$
\cite[Example~2.3]{JamneshanTaoFE}.

\begin{lemma}[Integrating a symmetric bilinear form; Lemma~2.4]
\label{lem:integratingFE}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] If $B:G\times G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is symmetric and
 globally bilinear, there is a globally quadratic map
 $\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in G$.
 \item[(ii)] Suppose in addition that $G$ is finite, $S\subset\widehat G$,
 and $\rho>0$.  If
 \[
  B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
 \]
 is symmetric and locally bilinear, then there is a radius $\rho'$ satisfying
 $\exp(-|S|^{O(1)})\rho\ll\rho'\leq\rho$,
 and a locally quadratic map
 $\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho')\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ for which
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ whenever
 $x,y\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho'/2)$.  Moreover, $\phi$ has a
 globally quadratic lift $\widetilde\phi:G_S\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ such
 that $\widetilde\phi(x,\theta)=\phi(x)$ whenever
 $\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}<\rho'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Extracting a locally linear frequency map}
\label{sec:lemma-2-5FE}

For the remainder, $G,f,\eta$ are as in Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}, and
$b(\,\cdot\,)$ denotes a changing $1$-bounded function.  The identity
\[
 \|f\|_{U^3(G)}^8
 =\mathbb{E}_{h\in G}\|\Delta_hf\|_{U^2(G)}^4,
 \qquad \Delta_hf(x)=f(x+h)\overline{f(x)},
\]
shows that for many increments $h$ there is a frequency
$\xi_h\in\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \left|\mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \Delta_hf(x)e(-\xi_h\cdot x)\right|\gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
We call such increments $h$ \emph{good}.  The next lemma organises their
frequencies into one locally linear map.

\begin{lemma}[Locally linear frequency map, Lemma~2.5 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:frequency-mapFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $1\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a locally linear map
$M:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\to\widehat G$, such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(h)b(x)f(x+h)e\bigl(-M(h)\cdot x\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

For each good increment $h$, choose a frequency $\xi_h$ at which
$\Delta_hf$ has a large Fourier coefficient.  The graph
$\Gamma=\{(h,\xi_h)\}\subset G\times\widehat G$ records how this frequency
depends on the direction $h$.  An additive quadruple in $\Gamma$ says that
the selected frequencies satisfy the same additive relation as their
directions, so many such quadruples suggest that $h\mapsto\xi_h$ behaves like
an approximate affine map.

Cauchy--Schwarz supplies these quadruples.  The theorem of Balog,
Szemer\'edi and Gowers isolates a large piece of the graph, and Pl\"unnecke's
inequalities control its iterated sums and differences.  After a refinement,
$4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''$ is still a graph
\cite[Propositions~5.1, 5.4, and~9.1]{GreenTaoFE}.  Bogolyubov places a
bounded-rank Bohr set in the projection of $2\Gamma''-2\Gamma''$
\cite[Lemma~6.3]{GreenTaoFE}.  The graph property gives each point of this
Bohr set a unique frequency $M(h)$ and forces $M$ to be locally linear.
Regularisation and pigeonholing then recover the displayed correlation
\cite[Proposition~9.3]{GreenTaoFE}.  For arbitrary torsion, Jamneshan and Tao
avoid the factor $2$ used in the odd-order argument.

\subsection{Symmetrising the local bilinear form}
\label{sec:lemma-2-6FE}

The form $(h,k)\mapsto M(h)\cdot k$ is locally bilinear but need not be
symmetric, whereas a quadratic second derivative must be symmetric.  The
next lemma corrects this while preserving the correlation.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric locally bilinear form, Lemma~2.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:symmetric-formFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $\eta^{O(1)}\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a symmetric
locally bilinear map
\[
 B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\times
   \operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
\]
such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h,k\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(x,h)b(x,k)f(x+h+k)e\bigl(-B(h,k)\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The symmetry argument of \cite[Lemma~9.4]{GreenTaoFE}, with the odd-order
factor $2$ omitted, makes $M(x)\cdot z-M(z)\cdot x$ uniformly small on a
smaller regular Bohr set.  Taking its midpoint with $M(z)\cdot x$ defines a
symmetric locally bilinear $B$.  Averaging the preceding correlation over
small shifts and expanding $M$ leaves the coupled phase $M(y)\cdot z$; it may
be replaced by $B(y,z)$ at small cost, while one-variable factors are absorbed
into $b$.  Pigeonholing gives the stated correlation, now ready for
integration.

\subsection{From bilinear structure to quadratic correlation}
\label{sec:theorem-1-6FE}

We now have exactly the object needed to recover a quadratic phase.  After
shrinking to a regular Bohr set, Lemma~\ref{lem:integratingFE} lifts $B$ to
$G_S$, integrates it there, and descends the result to a locally quadratic
map $\phi$ satisfying
\[
 B(y,z)=\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z).
\]
The lift is precisely what removes any restriction on the torsion of $G$.

It remains to expose $\phi$ in the correlation.  Choose a still smaller
regular Bohr set and average Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-formFE} after the change
$(h,k)\mapsto(h+y+z,k-y)$; regularity makes the resulting error negligible.
Local bilinearity separates all terms in $B(h+y+z,k-y)$ except $B(y,z)$.
The separated phases are absorbed into the changing functions $b$, while
the identity above converts $B(y,z)$ into quadratic phases.  After setting
$x'=x+h+k$ and pigeonholing $h,k$, one obtains a large trilinear average
whose integrand is
\[
 b(x',y+z)b(x',y)f(x'+z)e(-\phi(z)).
\]
For many base points $x'$, the corresponding $(y,z)$-average is large.  The
local Fourier lemma
\cite[Lemma~4.4]{GreenTaoFE} then supplies a frequency $\xi(x')$ for which
$f(x'+z)e(-\phi(z)-\xi(x')\cdot z)$ has large mean on the Bohr set.
Averaging over $x'$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.
