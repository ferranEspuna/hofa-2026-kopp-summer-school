\talktitle{The inverse theorem for the \texorpdfstring{$U^3$}{U3} Gowers
uniformity norm\texorpdfstring{\\}{} on arbitrary finite abelian groups: Fourier-analytic approach}
\talkafter{A. Jamneshan and T. Tao}~\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}
\talkspeaker{Ferran Espuña}{UAM--ICMAT}

\setcounter{equation}{0}

\begin{abstract}
Jamneshan and Tao prove an inverse theorem for the $U^3$ Gowers norm that
applies uniformly to every finite abelian group.  Their quantitative
Fourier-analytic theorem shows that a bounded function with large $U^3$ norm
correlates locally with quadratic structure, extending earlier results for
groups of odd order and for vector spaces over $\mathbb{F}_2$.  They also give
a nilmanifold formulation and a qualitative inverse theorem by ergodic
methods.  We overview only the Fourier-analytic argument, emphasizing the new
step that lifts a locally bilinear form to a global form on a larger group,
integrates it there, and descends the resulting quadratic phase.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Introduction}\label{sec:introductionFE}

The preceding talks highlighted the connection between additive structure
and arithmetic progressions: Talk~1 concerned Roth's theorem, Talks~8 and~9
returned to three-term progressions, and Talk~10 presented Gowers's argument
for four-term progressions \cite{GowersFourAPFE}.
For four-term progressions in the standard non-degenerate setting, repeated
applications of the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality control the progression-counting
operator by a $U^3$ norm.  Consequently, if a dense set $A$ of density $\alpha$
contains no non-trivial four-term arithmetic progressions, then its balanced
function $1_A-\alpha$ has large $U^3$ norm.
Identifying the structure of functions with 
large $U^3$ norm provides a key input to a density-increment iteration.

The main theorem of \cite{JamneshanTaoFE} provides precisely this structural input.
If a $1$-bounded function $f$ on a finite abelian group $G$ has large
$U^3(G)$ norm, then, averaged over base points $x$, the function
$h\mapsto f(x+h)$ on one controlled Bohr set correlates with a fixed locally
quadratic phase, up to a linear frequency $\xi(x)$ depending on $x$.  All
quantitative parameters depend only on the lower bound for the $U^3$ norm and
not on the group $G$.
This conclusion had essentially been obtained by Green and Tao when $|G|$
is odd \cite[Theorem~2.7]{GreenTaoFE}, and by Samorodnitsky when
$G=\mathbb{F}_2^n$ \cite[Theorem~2.3]{SamorodnitskyFE}.  Both proofs use
quadratic Fourier analysis, but a crucial integration step exploits the
special form of the group.

Write $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$,
$\xi\cdot x=\xi(x)$, and $e(\theta)=e^{2\pi i\theta}$.  For a finite
$S\subset\widehat G$, put
\[
 \|x\|_S=\sup_{\xi\in S}\|\xi\cdot x\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)=\{x\in G:\|x\|_S<\rho\}.
\]
A Bohr set is \emph{regular} if a small relative change in its radius produces
only a comparably small relative change in its cardinality.
For a map $\phi:U\to H$, where $U$ lies in an additive group, define its
additive derivative by $\partial_h\phi(x)=\phi(x)-\phi(x-h)$ whenever both
terms are defined.  The map is \emph{locally linear} if every second
derivative vanishes on its natural domain, and \emph{locally quadratic} if
every third derivative does.
Here and below, the constants implicit in $O(1)$ and in Vinogradov notation
are absolute.

\begin{theorem}[Locally quadratic inverse theorem, Theorem~1.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{thm:inverse-u3FE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, let $0<\eta\leq1/2$, and let
$f:G\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|f|\leq1$ and $\|f\|_{U^3(G)}\geq\eta$.  Then there
are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ satisfying
\[
 |S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)},
 \qquad
 \exp\bigl(-\eta^{-O(1)}\bigr)\ll\rho\leq\frac{1}{100},
\]
a locally quadratic map
$\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, and a map
$\xi:G\to\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \left|
  \mathbb{E}_{h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}
  f(x+h)e\bigl(-\phi(h)-\xi(x)\cdot h\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The Fourier-analytic proof is organised as follows.  The structural results
in Lemmas~\ref{lem:globalisingFE} and~\ref{lem:integratingFE}, collected in
Section~\ref{sec:bilinear-formsFE}, are used as black boxes and illustrated by
the cyclic lifting model.  Starting from the large $U^3$ norm,
Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-5FE} uses Fourier analysis and additive-combinatorial
input to construct a frequency map $M$ that is locally linear on a controlled
Bohr set.  Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-6FE} replaces the locally bilinear form
$(h,x)\mapsto M(h)\cdot x$ by a symmetric locally bilinear form $B$ while
preserving a quantitative correlation with $f$.  Finally,
Section~\ref{sec:theorem-1-6FE} globalises and integrates $B$.  The resulting
identity $B(y,z)=\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z)$ converts the bilinear phase into a
locally quadratic one, after which local Fourier analysis selects the
base-point-dependent frequency $\xi(x)$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.

\subsection{Globalising and integrating locally bilinear forms}
\label{sec:bilinear-formsFE}

The symmetry argument below produces a bilinear form defined only locally on
a Bohr set, whereas the usual integration of a bilinear form is a global
algebraic operation.  The two results in this section bridge that gap: first
the group and the form are lifted to a larger group $G_S$, where bilinearity
is global, and then the symmetric global form is integrated and restricted
back to a smaller Bohr set.  They are the structural input used in the final
step of the proof.

Let $U$ be a subset of an additive group and let $H$ be another additive
group.  A map $B:U\times U\to H$ is \emph{locally bilinear} if
$B(x_1+x_2,y)=B(x_1,y)+B(x_2,y)$ whenever all four arguments belong to $U$,
and likewise in the second variable.  It is \emph{symmetric} if
$B(x,y)=B(y,x)$.  For
$S\subset\widehat G$, define the lifted group
\[
 G_S=\left\{(x,\theta)\in G\times\mathbb{R}^S:
       (\xi\cdot x)_{\xi\in S}=\theta\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^S}\right\},
 \qquad
 \|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}=\sup_{\xi\in S}|\theta_\xi|.
\]
The following two results from
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE} will be used as black boxes.

\begin{lemma}[Globalising a locally bilinear form; Lemma~2.2]
\label{lem:globalisingFE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $S\subset\widehat G$, and
$0<\rho<1/2$.  If $B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\to H$ is locally
bilinear, then there is a globally bilinear map
$\widetilde B:G_S^2\to H$ such that
$\widetilde B((x,\theta),(y,\sigma))=B(x,y)$ whenever
$\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S},\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}
 \leq\exp(-C|S|^C)\rho$ for some absolute $C>0$.  If $B$ is symmetric,
$\widetilde B$ may also be chosen symmetric.
\end{lemma}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The cyclic lifting model
\cite[Example~2.3]{JamneshanTaoFE}.}
Let $G=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$, let $0<\rho<1/4$, and consider the character
$\psi([n])=n/N\pmod{1}$.  Set $S=\{\psi\}$.  Every element $x$ of
$\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ has a unique integer representative
$\widetilde{x}$ satisfying $|\widetilde{x}|<\rho N$.  For
$\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$, the map
\[
 B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},
 \qquad B(x,y)=\alpha\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y},
\]
is locally bilinear, but in general it cannot be extended to a globally
bilinear map on $G$: reduction modulo $N$ has discarded the carry information.
Instead consider the infinite cyclic group
\[
 G_S
 =\left\{\left([n],\frac{n}{N}\right):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
 \leq \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}.
\]
The formula
\[
 \widetilde{B}\left(\left([n],\frac{n}{N}\right),
                     \left([m],\frac{m}{N}\right)\right)=\alpha nm
\]
is globally bilinear and agrees with $B$ whenever
$|n|,|m|<\rho N$.  The extra real coordinate records every carry rather than
losing it, illustrating why the proof passes to $G_S$ instead of trying to
extend $B$ inside the original finite group.

Thus the lift removes the obstruction to global extension.  The next result
integrates the lifted symmetric form to a quadratic map and then descends it
to the original group.

\begin{lemma}[Integrating a symmetric bilinear form; Lemma~2.4]
\label{lem:integratingFE}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] If $B:G\times G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is symmetric and
 globally bilinear, there is a globally quadratic map
 $\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in G$.
 \item[(ii)] Suppose in addition that $G$ is finite, $S\subset\widehat G$,
 and $\rho>0$.  If
 \[
  B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
 \]
 is symmetric and locally bilinear, then there is a radius $\rho'$ satisfying
 \linebreak
 $\exp(-|S|^{O(1)})\rho\ll\rho'\leq\rho$,
 and a locally quadratic map
 $\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho')\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ for which
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ whenever
 $x,y\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho'/2)$.  Moreover, $\phi$ has a
 globally quadratic lift $\widetilde\phi:G_S\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ such
 that $\widetilde\phi(x,\theta)=\phi(x)$ whenever
 $\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}<\rho'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

In Section~\ref{sec:theorem-1-6FE}, part~(ii) will be applied to the symmetric
form produced in Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-6FE}.  Its proof uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:globalisingFE} to pass to $G_S$, part~(i) to integrate there,
and then restricts the resulting quadratic map back to $G$.

\subsection{Extracting a locally linear frequency map}
\label{sec:lemma-2-5FE}

For the remainder of the argument, $G$, $f$, and $\eta$ are as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.  Following the paper, $b(\,\cdot\,)$ denotes
an arbitrary $1$-bounded function of the displayed variables and may change
from one occurrence to the next.

This first stage turns the analytic assumption on $f$ into coherent
first-order Fourier structure.  Indeed, the recursive identity for the
Gowers norms,
\[
 \|f\|_{U^3(G)}^8
 =\mathbb{E}_{h\in G}\|\Delta_hf\|_{U^2(G)}^4,
 \qquad \Delta_hf(x)=f(x+h)\overline{f(x)},
\]
implies that many multiplicative derivatives have large $U^2$ norm.  Fourier
analysis therefore attaches a significant frequency to each of many
increments $h$; the point of the following statement is that these initially
unrelated frequencies can be organised into one locally linear map.

\begin{lemma}[Locally linear frequency map, Lemma~2.5 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:frequency-mapFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $1\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a locally linear map
$M:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\to\widehat G$, such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(h)b(x)f(x+h)e\bigl(-M(h)\cdot x\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

Here is how the additive-combinatorial input is used.  Choosing a large
Fourier coefficient $\xi_h$ of $\Delta_hf$ for each good $h$ gives a set
\[
 \Gamma=\{(h,\xi_h)\}\subset G\times\widehat G.
\]
Repeated Cauchy--Schwarz and Fourier inversion show that $\Gamma$ contains
many additive quadruples \cite[Proposition~5.1]{GreenTaoFE}.  The
Balog--Szemer\'edi--Gowers theorem extracts a large subset $\Gamma'$ whose
doubling is small, and Pl\"unnecke's inequalities then control all bounded
iterated sumsets and difference sets of $\Gamma'$; see
\cite[Theorems~5.2--5.3 and Proposition~5.4]{GreenTaoFE}.  A further
refinement gives a subgraph $\Gamma''$ for which
$4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''$ is still a graph, rather than a multivalued relation
\cite[Proposition~9.1]{GreenTaoFE}.

The Bogolyubov lemma then places a bounded-rank Bohr set inside the
set $2H''-2H''$ of first coordinates
\cite[Lemma~6.3]{GreenTaoFE}.  Because $2\Gamma''-2\Gamma''$ is a graph,
every $h$ in this Bohr set has a unique frequency $M(h)$; the graph property
for $4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''$ gives the local linearity of $M$.
Finally, regularising the Bohr set and pigeonholing a translate transfer the
original derivative correlations to this $M$, with fixed shifts and
frequencies absorbed into the functions $b$.  This is the content of
\cite[Proposition~9.3]{GreenTaoFE}.  Jamneshan and Tao make one essential
arbitrary-torsion modification: they write the graph directly as
$\{(h,M(h))\}$, instead of dividing its frequency coordinate by $2$ as in
the odd-order argument.  The finite-field model may also be found in
\cite{GreenFE}.  In fact, the regular radius at this stage can be chosen in
$[1/16,1/8]$; this is the meaning of $1\ll\rho\leq1/8$ in the statement.

This lemma is used in the next section by regarding
$(h,k)\mapsto M(h)\cdot k$ as a locally bilinear form.  Before it can be
integrated to a quadratic map, however, it must be made symmetric.

\subsection{Symmetrising the local bilinear form}
\label{sec:lemma-2-6FE}

The form $(h,k)\mapsto M(h)\cdot k$ is locally bilinear, but it need not be
symmetric.  Symmetry is indispensable because the second derivative of a
quadratic map is symmetric.  The next statement preserves the correlation
obtained above while replacing this form by a symmetric one.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric locally bilinear form, Lemma~2.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:symmetric-formFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $\eta^{O(1)}\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a symmetric
locally bilinear map
\[
 B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\times
   \operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
\]
such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h,k\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(x,h)b(x,k)f(x+h+k)e\bigl(-B(h,k)\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The symmetry argument of \cite[Lemma~9.4]{GreenTaoFE}, applied without the
factor of $2$ needed in the earlier odd-order formulation, first shows that
the antisymmetric part is controlled by the Bohr norm:
\[
 \bigl\|M(x)\cdot z-M(z)\cdot x\bigr\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}
 \ll \eta^{-O(1)}\|x\|_S.
\]
On a sufficiently smaller regular Bohr set this difference has a unique
small representative.  One can consequently take its midpoint, defining
$B$ and a small error $\Delta$ by
\[
 M(x)\cdot z=B(x,z)+\Delta(x,z),\qquad
 M(z)\cdot x=B(x,z)-\Delta(x,z).
\]
The map $B$ is symmetric by construction, and the local linearity of $M$
shows that it is locally bilinear.

To retain the correlation, average the conclusion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:frequency-mapFE} over small shifts $y,z$ and expand
$M(h+y)\cdot(x+z)$ using local linearity.  Every term depending on only one
of the displayed variables is absorbed into a $1$-bounded function $b$; the
only coupled phase left is $M(y)\cdot z$.  Since $\Delta(y,z)$ is uniformly
small, the triangle inequality permits replacing this phase by $B(y,z)$.
A change of variables followed by pigeonholing gives exactly the correlation
in Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-formFE}.  Thus this section supplies the symmetric
input required by Lemma~\ref{lem:integratingFE}.

\subsection{From bilinear structure to quadratic correlation}
\label{sec:theorem-1-6FE}

We now assemble the argument.  Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:integratingFE} to the
symmetric locally bilinear form $B$ obtained above, shrinking to a regular
Bohr set when necessary.  This produces a locally quadratic map $\phi$ with
\[
 B(y,z)=\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z).
\]
The globalising lemma is what makes this integration valid in arbitrary
torsion: inside the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:integratingFE}, $B$ is lifted to
$G_S$, integrated globally there, and then descended to the displayed local
identity on $G$.

Next, use regularity to average the correlation in
Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-formFE} over a small change of variables
$(h,k)\mapsto(h+y+z,k-y)$.  Bilinearity and the preceding identity give
\begin{align*}
 B(h+y+z,k-y)
  ={}&B(h,k)-B(h,y)+B(y+z,k)-B(y,y)\\
     &-\phi(y+z)+\phi(y)+\phi(z).
\end{align*}
All terms except $\phi(z)$ can be absorbed into the auxiliary functions
$b$.  After setting $x'=x+h+k$ and pigeonholing in $h,k$, one obtains, on
suitably chosen regular Bohr sets,
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x'\in G\\y,z}}
 b(x',y+z)b(x',y)f(x'+z)e\bigl(-\phi(z)\bigr)
 \right|\gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
Here $y$ ranges over a controlled Bohr set and $z$ over a smaller one; the
precise radii retain the bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.

The last step is local Fourier analysis.  For a positive proportion
$\gg\eta^{O(1)}$ of the base points $x'$, the inner $(y,z)$-average above is
large.  The large-trilinear-form lemma
\cite[Lemma~4.4]{GreenTaoFE} then selects a frequency
$\xi(x')\in\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \left|
  \mathbb{E}_{z\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho_*)}
  f(x'+z)e\bigl(-\phi(z)-\xi(x')\cdot z\bigr)
 \right|\gg\eta^{O(1)}
\]
for a regular radius
$\exp(-\eta^{-O(1)})\ll\rho_*\leq1/100$.  Define $\xi$ arbitrarily at the
remaining base points and average in $x'$.  This gives the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}: the combinatorial argument constructs a
locally linear derivative, symmetrisation turns it into an integrable
bilinear form, lifting and integration produce the quadratic phase, and
local Fourier analysis supplies the remaining linear twist.
