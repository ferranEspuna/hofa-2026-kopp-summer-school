\begin{abstract}
We outline Jamneshan and Tao's Fourier-analytic proof of their quantitative
$U^3$ inverse theorem for arbitrary finite abelian groups.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Introduction}\label{sec:introductionFE}

Earlier talks linked Gowers norms to arithmetic progressions.
In particular, Talk~10 bounded four-term progression counts by the $U^3$ norm
\cite{GowersFourAPFE}.
Essentially, if $A$ is a subset of a finite abelian group $G$
with density $\alpha$ and no nontrivial four-term arithmetic progressions,
then its balanced indicator function $f=1_A-\alpha$ has large $U^3$ norm,
and the inverse theorem turns this into
a structural description of $f$, 
which feeds back into a density increment argument in $\alpha$.
The theorem below is uniform in $G$.  Earlier proofs covered odd-order groups
\cite[Theorem~2.7]{GreenTaoFE} and $G=\mathbb{F}_2^n$
\cite[Theorem~2.3]{SamorodnitskyFE}, but relied on features unavailable in
arbitrary torsion.

Write $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$,
$\xi\cdot x=\xi(x)$, and $e(\theta)=e^{2\pi i\theta}$.  For a finite
$S\subset\widehat G$, put
\[
 \|x\|_S=\sup_{\xi\in S}\|\xi\cdot x\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)=\{x\in G:\|x\|_S<\rho\}.
\]
A Bohr set is \emph{regular} if a small relative change in its radius produces
only a comparably small relative change in its cardinality.
For a map $\phi:U\to H$, where $U$ lies in an additive group, define its
additive derivative by $\partial_h\phi(x)=\phi(x)-\phi(x-h)$ whenever both
terms are defined.  The map is \emph{locally linear} if every second
derivative vanishes on its natural domain, and \emph{locally quadratic} if
every third derivative does.

\begin{theorem}[Locally quadratic inverse theorem, Theorem~1.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{thm:inverse-u3FE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, let $0<\eta\leq1/2$, and let
$f:G\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|f|\leq1$ and $\|f\|_{U^3(G)}\geq\eta$.  Then there
are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ satisfying
\[
 |S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)},
 \qquad
 \exp\bigl(-\eta^{-O(1)}\bigr)\ll\rho\leq\frac{1}{100},
\]
a locally quadratic map
$\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, and a map
$\xi:G\to\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \left|
  \mathbb{E}_{h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}
  f(x+h)e\bigl(-\phi(h)-\xi(x)\cdot h\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Two algebraic black boxes enter near the end: globalisation of a locally
bilinear form on a lifted group $G_S$, and integration of a symmetric global
bilinear form to a quadratic map.  They are stated when used.  Suppressing
averages and auxiliary $1$-bounded factors, the obstruction evolves as
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \Delta_hf(x)e(-\xi_h\cdot x)
 \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
 f(x+h)e(-M(h)\cdot x)\\
 \rightsquigarrow\quad
 f(x+h+k)e(-B(h,k))
 \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad
 f(x+z)e(-\phi(z))\\
 \rightsquigarrow\quad
 f(x+z)e(-\phi(z)-\xi(x)\cdot z).
\end{gathered}
\]
Sections~\ref{sec:lemma-2-5FE}--\ref{sec:theorem-1-6FE} pass successively to
$M$, to a symmetric local form $B$ and its global lift, and to $\phi$ and the
final frequency.

\subsection{Extracting a locally linear frequency map}
\label{sec:lemma-2-5FE}

For the remainder, $G,f,\eta$ are as in Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}, and
$b(\,\cdot\,)$ denotes a changing $1$-bounded function.  The identity
\[
 \|f\|_{U^3(G)}^8
 =\mathbb{E}_{h\in G}\|\Delta_hf\|_{U^2(G)}^4,
 \qquad \Delta_hf(x)=f(x+h)\overline{f(x)},
\]
combined with the $U^2$ inverse theorem and averaging shows that for many
increments $h$ there is a frequency
$\xi_h\in\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \left|\mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \Delta_hf(x)e(-\xi_h\cdot x)\right|\gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
We call such increments $h$ \emph{good}.  At this stage $\xi_h$ may depend
arbitrarily on $h$; the next lemma organises the family into one locally
linear map.

\begin{lemma}[Locally linear frequency map, Lemma~2.5 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:frequency-mapFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $1\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a locally linear map
$M:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\to\widehat G$, such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(h)b(x)f(x+h)e\bigl(-M(h)\cdot x\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

For $\Gamma=\{(h,\xi_h)\}\subset G\times\widehat G$, additive quadruples
encode the same relations among the chosen frequencies as among their good
directions.

Cauchy--Schwarz supplies many such quadruples.  The theorem of Balog,
Szemer\'edi and Gowers isolates a large piece, while Pl\"unnecke controls its
iterated sums and differences.  After a refinement,
$4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''$ is still a graph
\cite[Propositions~5.1, 5.4, and~9.1]{GreenTaoFE}.  Here
\[
 4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''
 =\{\gamma_1+\cdots+\gamma_4-\gamma_5-\cdots-\gamma_8:
     \gamma_i\in\Gamma''\}.
\]
Saying that this set is a graph means that projection onto $G$ is injective:
if two such signed sums have the same first coordinate, then they also have
the same frequency coordinate.  Thus the resulting frequency depends only
on the combined increment, not on how that increment is represented.

Bogolyubov places a bounded-rank Bohr set in the projection of
$2\Gamma''-2\Gamma''$ \cite[Lemma~6.3]{GreenTaoFE}.  Consequently, each $h$
in this Bohr set has a unique frequency $M(h)$ with
$(h,M(h))\in2\Gamma''-2\Gamma''$.  If $h_1,h_2,h_1+h_2$ all lie in the Bohr
set, then
\[
 (h_1,M(h_1))+(h_2,M(h_2))
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 (h_1+h_2,M(h_1+h_2))
\]
may both be viewed as elements of $4\Gamma''-4\Gamma''$ with the same first
coordinate.  The graph property therefore gives
$M(h_1+h_2)=M(h_1)+M(h_2)$, which is precisely local linearity.
Regularisation and pigeonholing then recover the displayed correlation
\cite[Proposition~9.3]{GreenTaoFE}.
$M$ replaces the unrelated $\xi_h$, while $b$ absorbs the other factors:
the phase is now linear in $x$ and locally linear in $h$.

\subsection{Symmetrising and globalising the bilinear obstruction}
\label{sec:lemma-2-6FE}

For $U$ in an additive group and $H$ another, a map $B:U\times U\to H$ is
\emph{locally bilinear} if it is additive in each variable whenever all
points involved lie in $U$, and \emph{symmetric} if
$B(h,k)=B(k,h)$.  Normalise $M(0)=0$ by absorbing the constant frequency into
$b(x)$.  Then local linearity makes $M(h)\cdot k$ locally bilinear, and
averaging over a second increment exposes this phase.  The next lemma
symmetrises it so that it can be a quadratic second derivative.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric locally bilinear form, Lemma~2.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{lem:symmetric-formFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $\eta^{O(1)}\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a symmetric
locally bilinear map
\[
 B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\times
   \operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
\]
such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h,k\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(x,h)b(x,k)f(x+h+k)e\bigl(-B(h,k)\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The symmetry argument of \cite[Lemma~9.4]{GreenTaoFE}, with the odd-order
factor $2$ omitted, makes
$D(y,z)=M(y)\cdot z-M(z)\cdot y$ uniformly small on a smaller Bohr set.  Its
small real lift $d(y,z)$ is locally bilinear, so the midpoint
\[
 B(y,z)=M(y)\cdot z-\tfrac12d(y,z)
       =M(z)\cdot y+\tfrac12d(y,z)\pmod 1
\]
is symmetric and locally bilinear.  Averaging Lemma~\ref{lem:frequency-mapFE}
after small shifts in $h$ and $x$, then expanding $M$, leaves the coupled
phase $M(y)\cdot z$; one-variable terms enter $b$.  Replacing it by $B(y,z)$
and pigeonholing gives the displayed correlation.

The obstruction is now the fixed symmetric bilinear phase $e(-B(h,k))$, but
it is defined only on a Bohr set.  For $S\subset\widehat G$, introduce
\[
 G_S=\left\{(x,\theta)\in G\times\mathbb{R}^S:
       (\xi\cdot x)_{\xi\in S}=\theta\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^S}\right\},
 \qquad
 \|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}=\sup_{\xi\in S}|\theta_\xi|.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Globalising a locally bilinear form, Lemma~2.2 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]
\label{lem:globalisingFE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $S\subset\widehat G$, and
$0<\rho<1/2$.  If $B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\to H$ is locally
bilinear, then there is a globally bilinear map
$\widetilde B:G_S^2\to H$ such that
$\widetilde B((x,\theta),(y,\sigma))=B(x,y)$ whenever
$\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S},\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}
 \leq\exp(-C|S|^C)\rho$ for some absolute $C>0$.  If $B$ is symmetric,
$\widetilde B$ may also be chosen symmetric.
\end{lemma}

The lift is essential: even for a cyclic group, a locally bilinear form need
not extend to $G$ \cite[Example~2.3]{JamneshanTaoFE}.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:globalisingFE} to the form above, with
$H=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and $2\rho$ as the input radius, and set
$\rho_0=\exp(-C|S|^C)\rho$.  For
$h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho_0)$, let
$\widetilde h=(h,\theta_h)\in G_S$ be its canonical small lift, so that
$\|\theta_h\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}<\rho_0$.  The conclusion is a symmetric globally
bilinear map $\widetilde B:G_S^2\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying
\[
 \widetilde B(\widetilde h,\widetilde k)=B(h,k)
 \qquad
 (h,k\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho_0)).
\]
Thus, on the smaller Bohr set, the phase $e(-B(h,k))$ is literally the
restriction of the global bilinear phase $e(-\widetilde B)$ on $G_S$.
Global bilinearity is what allows the next lemma to integrate the obstruction.

\subsection{From bilinear structure to quadratic correlation}
\label{sec:theorem-1-6FE}

\begin{lemma}[Integrating a symmetric bilinear form, Lemma~2.4 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]
\label{lem:integratingFE}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] If $B:G\times G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is symmetric and
 globally bilinear, there is a globally quadratic map
 $\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in G$.
 \item[(ii)] Suppose in addition that $G$ is finite, $S\subset\widehat G$,
 and $\rho>0$.  If
 \[
  B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
 \]
 is symmetric and locally bilinear, then there is a radius $\rho'$ satisfying
 $\exp(-|S|^{O(1)})\rho\ll\rho'\leq\rho$,
 and a locally quadratic map
 $\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho')\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ for which
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ whenever
 $x,y\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho'/2)$.  Moreover, $\phi$ has a
 globally quadratic lift $\widetilde\phi:G_S\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ such
 that $\widetilde\phi(x,\theta)=\phi(x)$ whenever
 $\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}<\rho'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Part~(ii) records exactly this lift--integrate--restrict argument.  Applied to
Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-formFE}, it rewrites the obstruction as the second
derivative of a locally quadratic map $\phi$:
\[
 B(y,z)=\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z).
\]

Average Lemma~\ref{lem:symmetric-formFE} after
$(h,k)\mapsto(h+y+z,k-y)$; regularity controls the error.  Bilinearity leaves
as the only coupled $y,z$-phase
$e(B(y,z))=e(\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z))$.  The first two factors enter $b$,
leaving $e(-\phi(z))$.  Setting $x'=x+h+k$ and pigeonholing $h,k$ gives, on
regular Bohr sets $\mathcal B_2\subset\mathcal B_1$ chosen so that
$|\mathcal B_1+\mathcal B_2|\ll|\mathcal B_1|$,
\[
 \left|\mathbb{E}_{\substack{x'\in G\\y\in\mathcal B_1,\ z\in\mathcal B_2}}
 b(x',y+z)b(x',y)f(x'+z)e(-\phi(z))\right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
For many $x'$, the corresponding $(y,z)$-average is large.  The local
Fourier lemma \cite[Lemma~4.4]{GreenTaoFE} converts this final two-point
obstruction into a Fourier coefficient: it supplies a frequency $\xi(x')$
for which $f(x'+z)e(-\phi(z)-\xi(x')\cdot z)$ has large mean on $\mathcal B_2$.
Averaging over $x'$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.
