\begin{abstract}
Jamneshan and Tao prove an inverse theorem for the $U^3$ Gowers norm that
applies uniformly to every finite abelian group.  Their quantitative
Fourier-analytic theorem shows that a bounded function with large $U^3$ norm
correlates locally with quadratic structure, extending earlier results for
groups of odd order and for vector spaces over $\mathbb{F}_2$.  They also give
a nilmanifold formulation and a qualitative inverse theorem by ergodic
methods.  We overview only the Fourier-analytic argument, emphasizing the new
step that lifts a locally bilinear form to a global form on a larger group,
integrates it there, and descends the resulting quadratic phase.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Introduction}\label{sec:introductionFE}

The preceding talks highlighted the connection between additive structure
and arithmetic progressions: Talk~1 concerned Roth's theorem, Talks~8 and~9
returned to three-term progressions, and Talk~10 presented Gowers's argument
for four-term progressions \cite{GowersFourAPFE}.
For four-term progressions in the standard non-degenerate setting, repeated
applications of the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality control the progression-counting
operator by a $U^3$ norm.  Consequently, if a dense set $A$ of density $\alpha$
contains no non-trivial four-term arithmetic progressions, then its balanced
function $1_A-\alpha$ has large $U^3$ norm.
Identifying the structure of functions with 
large $U^3$ norm provides a key input to a density-increment iteration.

The main theorem of \cite{JamneshanTaoFE} provides precisely this structural
input.
If a $1$-bounded function $f$ on a finite abelian group $G$ has large
$U^3(G)$ norm, then, averaged over base points $x$, the function
$h\mapsto f(x+h)$ on one controlled Bohr set correlates with a fixed locally
quadratic phase, up to a linear frequency $\xi(x)$ depending on $x$.  All
quantitative parameters depend only on the lower bound for the $U^3$ norm and
not on the group $G$.
This conclusion had essentially been obtained by Green and Tao when $|G|$
is odd \cite[Theorem~2.7]{GreenTaoFE}, and by Samorodnitsky when
$G=\mathbb{F}_2^n$ \cite[Theorem~2.3]{SamorodnitskyFE}.  Both proofs use
quadratic Fourier analysis, but a crucial integration step exploits the
special form of the group.

Write $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$,
$\xi\cdot x=\xi(x)$, and $e(\theta)=e^{2\pi i\theta}$.  For a finite
$S\subset\widehat G$, put
\[
 \|x\|_S=\sup_{\xi\in S}\|\xi\cdot x\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)=\{x\in G:\|x\|_S<\rho\}.
\]
For a map $\phi:U\to H$, where $U$ lies in an additive group, define its
additive derivative by $\partial_h\phi(x)=\phi(x)-\phi(x-h)$ whenever both
terms are defined.  The map is \emph{locally linear} if every second
derivative vanishes on its natural domain, and \emph{locally quadratic} if
every third derivative does.

\begin{theorem}[Locally quadratic inverse theorem, Theorem~1.6 of
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE}]\label{thm:inverse-u3FE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, let $0<\eta\leq1/2$, and let
$f:G\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $|f|\leq1$ and $\|f\|_{U^3(G)}\geq\eta$.  Then there
are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ satisfying
\[
 |S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)},
 \qquad
 \exp\bigl(-\eta^{-O(1)}\bigr)\ll\rho\leq\frac{1}{100},
\]
a locally quadratic map
$\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$, and a map
$\xi:G\to\widehat G$ such that
\[
 \mathbb{E}_{x\in G}
 \left|
  \mathbb{E}_{h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}
  f(x+h)e\bigl(-\phi(h)-\xi(x)\cdot h\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Here and below, the constants implicit in $O(1)$ and in Vinogradov notation
are absolute.

The Fourier-analytic proof is organised as follows.  The structural results
in Lemmas~\ref{lem:globalisingFE} and~\ref{lem:integratingFE}, collected in
Section~\ref{sec:bilinear-formsFE}, are used as black boxes and illustrated by
the cyclic lifting model.  Starting from the large $U^3$ norm,
Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-5FE} uses Fourier analysis and additive-combinatorial
input to construct a frequency map $M$ that is locally linear on a controlled
Bohr set.  Section~\ref{sec:lemma-2-6FE} replaces the locally bilinear form
$(h,x)\mapsto M(h)\cdot x$ by a symmetric locally bilinear form $B$ while
preserving a quantitative correlation with $f$.  Finally,
Section~\ref{sec:theorem-1-6FE} globalises and integrates $B$.  The resulting
identity $B(y,z)=\phi(y+z)-\phi(y)-\phi(z)$ converts the bilinear phase into a
locally quadratic one, after which local Fourier analysis selects the
base-point-dependent frequency $\xi(x)$ in
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.

\subsection{Globalising and integrating locally bilinear forms}
\label{sec:bilinear-formsFE}

Let $U$ be a subset of an additive group and let $H$ be another additive
group.  A map $B:U\times U\to H$ is \emph{locally bilinear} if
$B(x_1+x_2,y)=B(x_1,y)+B(x_2,y)$ whenever all four arguments belong to $U$,
and likewise in the second variable.  It is \emph{symmetric} if
$B(x,y)=B(y,x)$.  For
$S\subset\widehat G$, define the lifted group
\[
 G_S=\left\{(x,\theta)\in G\times\mathbb{R}^S:
       (\xi\cdot x)_{\xi\in S}=\theta\pmod{\mathbb{Z}^S}\right\},
 \qquad
 \|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}=\sup_{\xi\in S}|\theta_\xi|.
\]
It fits into the short exact sequence
$0\to\mathbb{Z}^S\to G_S\to G\to0$.  The following two results from
\cite{JamneshanTaoFE} will be used as black boxes.

\begin{lemma}[Globalising a locally bilinear form; Lemma~2.2]
\label{lem:globalisingFE}
Let $G$ be a finite abelian group, $S\subset\widehat G$, and
$0<\rho<1/2$.  If $B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\to H$ is locally
bilinear, then there is a globally bilinear map
$\widetilde B:G_S^2\to H$ such that
$\widetilde B((x,\theta),(y,\sigma))=B(x,y)$ whenever
$\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S},\|\sigma\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}
 \leq\exp(-C|S|^C)\rho$ for some absolute $C>0$.  If $B$ is symmetric,
$\widetilde B$ may also be chosen symmetric.
\end{lemma}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{The cyclic lifting model
\cite[Example~2.3]{JamneshanTaoFE}.}
Let $G=\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$, let $0<\rho<1/4$, and consider the character
$\psi([n])=n/N\pmod{1}$.  Set $S=\{\psi\}$.  Every element $x$ of
$\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ has a unique integer representative
$\widetilde{x}$ satisfying $|\widetilde{x}|<\rho N$.  For
$\alpha\in\mathbb{R}$, the map
\[
 B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},
 \qquad B(x,y)=\alpha\widetilde{x}\widetilde{y},
\]
is locally bilinear, but in general it cannot be extended to a globally
bilinear map on $G$: reduction modulo $N$ has discarded the carry information.
Instead consider the infinite cyclic group
\[
 G_S
 =\left\{\left([n],\frac{n}{N}\right):n\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}
 \leq \mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{R}.
\]
The formula
\[
 \widetilde{B}\left(\left([n],\frac{n}{N}\right),
                     \left([m],\frac{m}{N}\right)\right)=\alpha nm
\]
is globally bilinear and agrees with $B$ whenever
$|n|,|m|<\rho N$.  The extra real coordinate records every carry rather than
losing it, illustrating why the proof passes to $G_S$ instead of trying to
extend $B$ inside the original finite group.

\begin{lemma}[Integrating a symmetric bilinear form; Lemma~2.4]
\label{lem:integratingFE}
Let $G$ be a finitely generated abelian group.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item[(i)] If $B:G\times G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ is symmetric and
 globally bilinear, there is a globally quadratic map
 $\phi:G\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ satisfying
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ for every $x,y\in G$.
 \item[(ii)] Suppose in addition that $G$ is finite, $S\subset\widehat G$,
 and $\rho>0$.  If
 \[
  B:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)^2\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
 \]
 is symmetric and locally bilinear, then there is a radius $\rho'$ satisfying
 \linebreak
 $\exp(-|S|^{O(1)})\rho\ll\rho'\leq\rho$,
 and a locally quadratic map
 $\phi:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho')\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ for which
 $\phi(x+y)=\phi(x)+\phi(y)+B(x,y)$ whenever
 $x,y\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho'/2)$.  Moreover, $\phi$ has a
 globally quadratic lift $\widetilde\phi:G_S\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ such
 that $\widetilde\phi(x,\theta)=\phi(x)$ whenever
 $\|\theta\|_{\mathbb{R}^S}<\rho'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Proof of Lemma 2.5}\label{sec:lemma-2-5FE}

For the remainder of the argument, $G$, $f$, and $\eta$ are as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:inverse-u3FE}.  Following the paper, $b(\,cdot\,)$ denotes
an arbitrary $1$-bounded function of the displayed variables and may change
from one occurrence to the next.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma~2.5]\label{lem:frequency-mapFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $1\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a locally linear map
$M:\operatorname{Bohr}(S,2\rho)\to\widehat G$, such that
\[
 \left|
 \mathbb{E}_{\substack{x\in G\\h\in\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)}}
 b(h)b(x)f(x+h)e\bigl(-M(h)\cdot x\bigr)
 \right|
 \gg\eta^{O(1)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

The additive-combinatorial input to this lemma---the
Balog--Szemer\'edi--Gowers theorem, Pl\"unnecke inequalities, and the
Bogolyubov lemma---was covered in Talks 3, 4, and 5 and will be used as a
black box.
The finite-field model of the argument can be found in \cite{GreenFE}.

\begin{lemma}[Lemma~2.6]\label{lem:symmetric-formFE}
There are a regular Bohr set $\operatorname{Bohr}(S,\rho)$ with
$|S|\ll\eta^{-O(1)}$ and $\eta^{O(1)}\ll\rho\leq1/8$, and a symmetric

\[

 \right|

\]
\end{lemma}
